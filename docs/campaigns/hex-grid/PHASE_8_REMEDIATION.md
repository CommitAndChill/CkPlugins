## Workload and diagnosis

The reference is the i9-10900KF workstation, two radius-32 boards with 3,169 cells each, 3,168 or 5,704 occupied objects in total, Win64 Development. Each qualification percentile is the largest of five measured fresh-process samples after a separate warmup. Use paired run-level intervals for subtraction. Summing stage percentiles produces an invalid critical path.

**Restore.** SaveKey resolver readiness to completion of entity mapping is the largest measured interval at both densities. All rows mapped in two rebuild frames. Removing this entire interval from each run would still leave p95 at 646/921 ms. At 90%, the median map browse alone took 170 ms, quarantine stamp to enqueue 129 ms, enqueue to lift 139 ms and lift to drain declaration 122 ms. The two board payloads occupied 8.19 MB and the 5,704 object identity payloads 0.94 MB in the slot. These byte counts identify decode workloads. The stage clocks also include other work on the same frames. [Authority waterfall, source and replay](evidence/phase-8-profiling/restore.md)

**Inside the 90% rebuild frame.** The cap-3 server CPU trace recorded a 1,174 ms GameThread frame. Three disjoint branches account for about 1,132 ms: scheduler MainPass 794 ms, PostActorConstruction 246 ms and SpawnActor 92 ms. Inside MainPass, 5,706 EntityScript spawn requests consumed 725 ms inclusive, including 167 ms in the CreateObject branch. PostActorConstruction includes 241 ms of mixed-object BeginPlay. Parent and child times must not be added. Start the authority investigation in EntityScript request handling and mixed-object initialization. The CreateObject scope includes the instancing-policy decision. The offline commandlets wrote timing exports but returned failure on memory tags. Frame-index and exclusive-time reconciliation support these GameThread timings only. **Join.** On the 50 ms RTT, 10 Mbps shaped loopback, the server spent approximately 3.8/6.9–7.0 seconds sending about 0.69 Mbps in groups of three datagrams around 33 ms apart. The plateau grew with occupancy. At 90%, all downstream wire bytes through the observed receipt amounted to about 724 ms of ideal serialization at line rate. That includes handshake and other connection traffic and uses a different start from the release clock. It is a capacity check, not an additive component of observed-open latency. ### Paired diagnostic on the qualification PC

On the same staged Game and Server, the 90% diagnostic with CPU tracing and a server-only Iris packet cap of 3 took **7,599.94 ms** from observed open to verified client state. Setting the cap to 12 took **1,891.19 ms**, a 5,709 ms reduction in this pair. Both servers logged the requested value. The regular bursts changed from three to twelve packets, with approximately 0.69 and 2.77 Mbps respectively. Downstream modeled wire volume through the receipt was 905,161 and 904,479 B. The matching state within each run rules out a smaller verified workload as the explanation. One pair at one density identifies a pacing contribution. It establishes neither a release p95 nor the best cap. Both cases still missed the join budget and had the recorded post-cutoff delivery and inspector diagnostics. [Packet and client analysis](evidence/phase-8-profiling/join.md) · [Cap 3 receipt and trace hashes](evidence/phase-8-profiling/cap-3-receipt.json) · [Cap 12 receipt and trace hashes](evidence/phase-8-profiling/cap-12-receipt.json)

The client CPU trace also shows a cost to increasing the cap. The worst GameThread frame in the aligned diagnostic window rose from **106.0 ms to 192.2 ms**. Exclusive Iris object-read time was about 156/158 ms across the two windows, EntityScript spawn request time was 216/213 ms, and replicated-fragment dispatch was 134/175 ms (cap 3/12). Faster arrival concentrated work into fewer frames. These are two single-run maxima. Add a join-specific frame-tail and established-client fairness decision to R2B before selecting a cap. The established 1 ms grid preview/move measurement covers a different workload.

At 90%, the release cohort needs 1,753 ms of authority savings and 6,602 ms of join savings at p95. The faster join diagnostic still needs 891 ms to reach the endpoint budget. For each proposed change, report the *maximum recoverable wall time* from the measured critical path and the expected effect on overlapping work. This prevents an expensive subsystem rewrite with too little headroom to close the gap.

**Review constraints.** The dense workload uses actor-backed mixed grid objects with both square and hex composition. Keep that path as the qualified workload. Saved actor fields must be present before FinishSpawning and BeginPlay, construction must complete before hydration, and the full mapped set must pass quarantine and convergence before publication. The board payload deliberately clears physical occupant handles. Preserve independently arriving object binding keys, transfer/relevance and teardown. An optimized path needs the same observable results from forward and reverse lookups on live objects. An actor-free floor experiment can bound costs but cannot qualify the original case.

Performance configuration belongs to the diagnostic runner during these experiments. Board data, footprint authoring and gym visuals have no proposed interface changes. The CkTests gym should show separate pending and usable states during travel/rejoin, while automation proves identity and timing from public queries. Publishing a board or skipping manifest verification early would change the benchmark contract.
